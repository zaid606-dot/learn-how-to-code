# Strech redesign: BRIEF

Spec redesign of strech.ai, built locally to pitch to the owners. Interviewed
(answers below are the requester's words, lightly trimmed). Content and sample
figures come from two screen recordings of the live site; the live site itself
was not reachable from the build environment.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** "Premium and trusted." "More editorial rather than a bunch of
   different AI-generated assets." "More premium and more interactive for the
   user to get hooked on."
2. **Journey, in their words.** "They first get to the page, and it's some sort
   of extremely indulging hook ... showing how tapping the app gets them a quick
   outcome. It's super fast and just a quick hook." Then calm sections covering
   what is already on the site.
3. **Energy curve.** "The beginning is the most intense." "It should feel calm
   after that first part when you scroll down."
4. **Feeling and the one moment.** "The advancement of the website and how it
   reflects the company and how advanced and professional it is."
5. **What no other site does.** "Be more advanced ... The owners should see this
   and think, 'Wow, I'm going to pay $10,000 for this. I've never seen anything
   like this.'"
6. **Range.** Editorial, premium.
7. **One world or scenes.** Distinct scenes: "not like a world that we'd be
   flying through."
8. **Assets.** Everything on the live site: logo, colours, copy. Logo and name
   unchanged ("Strech", no flip). Zero spend: no generated imagery.

## Product truth (from the recordings)

Strech is pre-launch appliance-repair dispatch: homeowners describe a fault,
see a price range, get matched with a vetted local pro, approve one fixed
itemized quote, and track the visit live. Pros get matched jobs, set their own
price band, quote in-app and get paid in-app. Primary action: **Join the
waitlist** (pros: **Join the pro waitlist**). Next: home tracking ("health you
can read like a plant") and predictive dispatch, both coming soon.

Sample job used on the live site and reused here, labelled as a sample:
dishwasher, won't drain, Sofia M. 4.9, van #204, arriving 9:42, estimate
$180 to $260, quote $240 (drain pump $95, labor 1.5 hr $145, diagnostic $89,
diagnostic credit minus $89), 90-day workmanship warranty. No usage statistics
exist, so none are shown.

## Grammar

Chaptered editorial. Hard-cut grounds per chapter, folio in the margin instead
of a fixed bar, title-page hero with no media above the fold, colophon close
with the CTA set as running text.

## Feeling curve

| Chapter | Feeling | Caused by |
|---|---|---|
| Dispatch (title page) | Thrill, "did that just work?" | Tapping the headline dispatches a pro inside the sentence |
| Your words are enough | Ease | Quiet spread, the fault in plain words |
| The quote is the invoice | Certainty | The receipt itemizes itself and lands inside the range |
| Know where the help is | Calm watching | The visit advances along the vine as you read |
| No job too small | Lightness | A light paper spread of small tasks |
| For pros | Respect | The page turns green: the other side of the market |
| The whole garden | Wonder, the quiet second high | Appliances as plants; one wilts and is tended early |
| Colophon | Resolved | Small type, an inline waitlist field, the vine ends in a bud |

## The peak

"I tapped the headline and it actually sent someone: a name, a van, an arrival
time and a price, in two seconds." Lives in the title page. The peak is
tap-driven, so its room is time, not scroll span.

## Tell-someone sentence

It's the site where you tap the headline and a repair pro gets dispatched
inside the sentence, then the job ticket follows you down the page until the
repair is done.

## Signature move

The running job ticket on a growing vine: a margin folio that is also the job
ticket for the sample repair, advancing Requested, Matched, Quoted $240,
Approved, On the way, Done, Paid as chapters pass, on a vine that grows down
the page and ends in a bud at the close.

## Authored silence

None. Every chapter carries content.
